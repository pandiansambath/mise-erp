# KAL · கல் — the temple design of the main page
Art direction and storyboard. 25 Nov 2026. (DesignId `kovil`; the name shown in the picker: **Kal · கல்**.)

> In Tamil one word, **கல்**, says *stone*. Said to somebody, the same word says *learn*.
> The page is built on that word: it is all stone, it is all learning, and it is the last thing cut in it.

How to read this. Sizes are CSS px on two screens: **D** = 1536×730 (his laptop), **P** = 393×852 (his phone;
it must also hold at 393×740, with the address bar showing). `z` is px toward the viewer; 0 is the plane the text
lies in. 1 **LU** (light unit) = 140 px = one unit of awardHall.ts's light maths, so its constants carry over.
"The hand" = pointer or finger. "The torch" = the hand's light. `code` words are Template keys; those that begin
`temple.` are new (§11). P0 = the design does not exist without it, P1 = it is not what he asked for without it, P2 = riches.

What I looked at: the award room closed and open (D), closed (P), its leaves (D, P), its zoomed strip, the present
main page (D, P). What is on them that he loves: one warm light raking over deep carving; black that is never empty
because something gold is always on its edge; a real object with a real sound; letters that mean something; and
text on leaf that is simply *legible*. What is weak on them, not to be inherited: the closed room is about 70 % dead
black; everything is one brown; the awake Kural is drawn as outlines (he saw "gaps inside the words", 107.5); on D the
left and right quarters hold nothing; light serif capitals at 13 px over relief are hard to read.

# 1 · ONE CONCEPT

## 1.1 Two weighed
**A. Walk in through halls.** A camera goes forward through doorway after doorway to the chest. The strongest sense of
place, and it hands over to /award by itself. But the words must be scaled and moved in 3-D on every frame (blurred,
re-rastered: exactly 106.9), nine halls are nine walls of unique art, and on a phone a corridor is a thin strip.

**B. Go down the face of a temple cut from one rock.** The old way: Vettuvan Koil at Kazhugumalai (a Pandya work, about
1,200 years old) was cut out of a hill **from the top downward**, and its foot was never freed from the rock. Scroll
direction = carving direction. Text and stone are one sheet, so text is always sharp; there can be no cut, because it
is one wall. The risk is a flat elevation: answered with real overhangs, recesses, hanging things and foreground rock.

**Decision: B.** It is the only one in which the scroll itself *means* something, and the only one that keeps his words sharp.

## 1.2 The idea
At dusk you come to the top of a hill. In it somebody is cutting a temple, from the top down, out of one rock. You carry
a lamp. You go down its face. What you pass is finished; what is below you is still rock, with the masons' red lines
drawn on it, and it **lets go as your light comes**. Lamps you have lit stay lit. The further down, the rawer the stone
and the wetter it gets. At the bottom the carving stops in the rock, one link of a chain is half freed, and there is water.

## 1.3 One word, one couplet
- **The couplet.** Thirukkural 396 is the page. Its first line is cut at the very top, under his face; its second at the very
  bottom, at the water's edge. Everything he is lies between the two lines.
  `தொட்டனைத் தூறும் மணற்கேணி மாந்தர்க்குக்` / `கற்றனைத் தூறும் அறிவு` (396, the chapter on learning, the same as his 400.)
  In English, on the stone: *Dig deeper in sand, and more water comes; / learn more, and more is known.*
- **The word.** On the last uncut block: **கல்**. Your light on it: STONE. Keep it there: LEARN. And while the water under it
  lies still, its reflection reads LEARN by itself. (His own finale says "Just keep learning".)
- **The chain.** Temple masons cut hanging chains of loose links out of a single stone. His months are such a chain, and his
  own words are already its caption: "One link at a time. Every day is a link, and the chain is still being made." (`finaleNote`)

## 1.5 Six materials, six meanings (this IS the design system; never break it)
| material | means | so |
|---|---|---|
| **stone** | what has been done | headings, facts, the wall |
| **gold leaf** | what was earned | his name, honours, the rank. Never a button |
| **palm leaf** | what it means | every explanation, every detail that opens, every hint |
| **copper and bronze** | what you can press or take | buttons, chips, links, résumé, menus |
| **flame** | where you are, what is live | focus, "LIVE", progress, visited lamps |
| **water** | what keeps moving | messages, the clock's seconds, the finale |

A visitor learns it without being told: metal answers a press, a leaf's corner sticking out of the stone can be pulled,
a flame marks the place. "Boxes that look alike" cannot happen, because a thing's material is its function.

## 1.6 The masons' marvels (each a real feat of Tamil masons; each does something under the hand)
1. The stone **chain** from one block (S2) · 2. the **turning wheel** (S1) · 3. the **face in the horseshoe window** (kudu; S1)
· 4. **pillars that sing** (S4–S6) · 5. **pierced windows** (S6b) · 6. the **stone ball loose in the yali's mouth** (S7)
· 7. **copper plates on a sealed ring** (S3) · 8. the **bull and the elephant that share one head** (S9: lit from the left it is
the one, from the right the other) · 9. the **single unbroken kolam line** (S4b) · 10. **stepped tank** (S11). Ours are our own drawings.

## 1.7 Two fish
The seal of the Pandyas was two fish, and his name is Pandian. The pair is this design's mark: on the copper ring's seal (S3),
at the crown of the treasury door (S7), and alive in the water (S11). It is a mark for a *name*; the page never claims descent.

## 1.8 Truth and respect
Architecture, animals, plants, letters, light, water. No deity, idol, priest, worshipper, offering, sect mark (no three white
bars, no U/Y marks), Om, conch, trident, serpent stone, flagstaff, bali stone, guardian figures (dvarapalas: yalis stand there
instead), no kirtimukha face (a lotus bud crowns each kudu). No sanctum: the innermost place is a **treasury**. No Kural from the
first chapter, none that names a god or fate. The water is a spring, never "holy". Nothing is struck, broken or run for a joke:
rock comes off because a mason frees what is inside it. Animals are carved, never cute; nothing has a smile drawn on it.

## 1.9 How it stands up (so that it can be built, smooth, on his two machines)
1. **One canvas, and it rides with the page.** It is absolute inside the page (not fixed): 100lvh + 160 px above and below. When
   the scroll has moved, one rAF sets its `translateY` and draws. Between two frames the browser itself carries it together with
   the text, so **stone and text can never slide against each other** (a fixed canvas swims in a fling). Only the 160 px ahead of
   the motion is drawn besides the view (+22 % while scrolling, 0 at rest); beyond it lies `#0b0908`.
2. **The DOM is the architect.** The page is laid out as ordinary HTML; each stone thing is declared on it (`data-stone="slab rope"`,
   `"eave"`, `"lintel"`…), measured by ResizeObservers only, and built round it: slabs and frames are 9-slices, bands tile, pictures
   are fixed. He edits a word: the wall follows.
3. **Camera: 2.5-D.** For a vertex p (px): `screen = c + o + (p.xy − c − o·k)·D/(D − p.z)` with c = the view's middle, D = 1100 (P 900), and the
   eye's offset o entering so that z = 0 never moves: `p.xy + o·p.z/(D − p.z)`. o = ±70/±40 px from the pointer (P: ±46/±30 from tilt),
   eased 0.5 s. Going down past a ledge at z = 56 you see its top, then its underside: both faces are modelled.
4. **Shading.** awardHall's wall shader (slopes, openness, worn edges, self-shadow march 7 (P 5), parallax), per instance: torch + its 3 nearest
   lamps (indices worked out at layout, not in the pixel) + ambient + moon. Variants: wall, polish, metal, water, sand, flame, shard.
5. **The rock that lets go** (the signature; not a fade, not a noise dissolve). A baked tile holds Voronoi cells (id, distance to edge).
   Raw rock stands 26 px proud (P 18) over the carving. Cell time = 0.55·distance from the strike + 0.30·noise + 0.15·height. A cell's last
   90 ms: its edges go dark, it tips 8°; then it is gone and a shard of its size falls (instanced plates; D 96, P 48 alive at most; gravity
   1150 px/s², spin 90–260°/s, lost behind the next ledge within 0.9 s). The new edge is pale fresh granite for 1.2 s. One puff of dust to
   six shards. 1.15 s a scene (P 0.95). Outside a scene that is being cut the branch costs nothing.
   The **slabs that carry text are never under rock**: they are polished and readable before, during and after.
6. **Masons' red lines** on raw rock are drawn from the modules' own outlines (they know where they will stand), so they are always true.
7. **Stone under a fast hand** (his honeycomb scatter, in stone): flow.ts's numbers as a small texture; where stirred, honeycomb cells
   (r 11 px, P 9) lift, drift with the water, show the dark under them, and settle in 1.6 s with a trickle of sand. Wall, cut words and the
   portrait do it; text-safe rects do not. No backdrop-filter, no second canvas.
8. **Budget.** Contexts: 1. D 1920×912 px drawn: ≤ 11 ms GPU on the UHD 620; P 1081×2343: ≤ 12 ms. ≤ 60 draws, ≤ 90 k triangles. JS ≤ 3 ms a scroll
   frame, no layout read, no per-frame style on an element that carries its own look. Overdraw ≤ 1.6. Textures ≤ 85 MB on the GPU (P 42), two pages
   of carving resident at most; download ≤ 2.9 MB whole, ≤ 1.0 MB for S1 (P 1.6 / 0.55). At rest 30 fps, and only flames, torch pool and moving
   parts; covered by a popup or hidden: 0. If the meter says no: bake each LIT scene's lamplight once into its own picture at full sharpness and
   shade only the torch's pool live. Never lower the resolution.
9. **The static twin** (§2 "still"): with no WebGL, lost context or reduced motion the same DOM wears lit pictures of the same carvings (border-image
   slabs, strips, stills), all lamps lit. The hero's still is also the first paint, lit by the same rig, so the canvas takes over unseen.

**Reading rules (hold everywhere).** (1) Words are drawn **once**: either cut letters in the canvas (the DOM twin is there, transparent,
selectable, with a visible `::selection`) or DOM text with nothing of it in the canvas; never both. (2) DOM text only on slab, leaf,
copper or sand, never on carving. (3) Under a text-safe rect (the text's box + 14 px) the stone's luminance is capped at 0.026 (sRGB
≤ 48) whatever the torch does: ivory on it is never under 11:1, the faintest ink never under 5.6:1. (4) No text-shadow on slabs.
(5) Nothing tilts, turns or scales while it carries text, except the month's and Kural's leaves on their way in (0.6 s).
(6) Cut letters are **filled** with gold in every state; the rim only adds to the fill (§4).

**States of a scene**: RAW (rock, red lines; slabs already polished, one mason's lamp in the rail) → CUT (the rock has let go: by itself
when the scene's top passes 72 % of the view, at once where the hand touches) → LIT (its lamps catch one after another 0.5 s later, or
as the torch comes within 110 px) → AWAKE (all its lamps lit: gold leaf gets its full light, the picture begins its own quiet motion).
Kept for the visit (sessionStorage). A jump by the index finishes a scene in 0.45 s. Nothing the visitor needs waits for any of it.

**Still** (no WebGL, reduced motion): as AWAKE, no motion, torch off; described once here: every picture has a lit still; chains hang
straight; the Kural shows Tamil with its English under it in `Small`; the leaf still opens.

## 5.3 Cut letters and real text
A cut word is a DOM element with `data-cut`; its text goes transparent **only after** the canvas has drawn its twin once, and a lost context gives it back its gold at once (glGuard). The twin: the same font, size and place, drawn by the browser to a canvas (so Tamil is shaped right), turned into distances in a worker (spread 10 px; one R8 sheet 2048×2560), made anew when he edits a word or a box changes size.
The cut: a V at 45°, its floor gilded. `fill = smoothstep(−.5, +.5 px)` of **solid** gold; an inner bevel of 1.6 px (P 1.2) lit by the same lamps; a dark contact line of 1 px on the shaded side; rim light adds at most 25 % of the fill. *Fill first, rim second*: with every effect off it must be a solid, plain word. (The award room's letters are rim first: that is 107.5.)

# 8 · DON'TS, AND THE TEN WAYS IT COULD LOOK CHEAP

## 8.1 Don'ts (this design)
No text on carving. No word drawn twice. No gold on anything that is only a button. No rounded rectangle, no glass, no blur, no card with a border on four sides and nothing to do with its picture. No fade-ups, no typewriter, no parallax on text, no tilt on hover. No `backdrop-filter`, no fixed full-screen layer, no second canvas. No photo turned to stone or sepia. No stock texture seen twice in a view at the same angle. No people, faces or hands carved (his photo is the only face). No gods (1.8). No "exotic" Latin letters, no paisley, no mandala wallpaper, no henna pattern, no sitar cliché in the sound. No lens flare, no god rays except through S6b's windows and S7's crack. No confetti of petals: petals only in S5b, and they have a job. No scroll hijack, no pinned scene. No hint at PS or at the photo.

# 10 · HOW TO TELL IT IS RIGHT
1. Twenty-two pictures (11 scenes × D, P), torch off and on, through the meter (3.4); twelve at P with 740 px of height.
2. Read every word at arm's length; then with the torch on it; then in two light themes; then with the canvas switched off.
